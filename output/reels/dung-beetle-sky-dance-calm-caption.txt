A compass check on top of a ball of dung: Kheper nigroaeneus at work.

Climbing up and spinning round lets this beetle fix a heading from the sky, so it can push its ball away along a straight path. On dark moonless nights, some species navigate using the glow of the Milky Way, which no other animal had been shown to do. Recorded in a South African research arena.

Source: Baird et al. 2012, PLoS ONE; Dacke et al. 2013, Current Biology.
