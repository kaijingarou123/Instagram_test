A hummingbird's heart can beat 1,260 times a minute. So how does it survive a cold night?

Torpor. It drops its body temperature and metabolism to a fraction of normal until morning: a nightly mini-hibernation. And a ruby-throated hummingbird can still fatten up and cross 800 km of the Gulf of Mexico in one flight.

Source: Cornell Lab of Ornithology (All About Birds). Photos: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0 (credits shown on screen).

Orcas in the Antarctic Peninsula have a hunting technique that looks like teamwork from a movie: they line up, charge an ice floe and dive at the last second, creating a wave that washes seals off the ice.

It's culture, not instinct. Only some pods do it, and young orcas take years to learn.

Source: Pitman & Durban (2012), Marine Mammal Science. Photos: Callan Carpenter via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0 / CC BY 4.0.

Snow leopards can't roar. Lions, tigers, jaguars and leopards have a pad of fibro-elastic tissue in the larynx that lets them rumble; the snow leopard doesn't, so it chuffs, growls and yowls instead.

The 'ghost of the mountains' lives at 3,000–4,500 m across 12 countries. Fewer than 10,000 mature adults remain (IUCN: Vulnerable).

Sources: Hast (1989) J. Anatomy; IUCN Red List 2017; Snow Leopard Trust. Photos: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0 (captive animals, Zoo Karlsruhe); credits on screen.
