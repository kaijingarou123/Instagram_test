How does a bear live on termites? The sloth bear (Melursus ursinus) is missing its top two front teeth, can seal its nostrils shut, and feeds so noisily it can be heard up to 185 m away.

It rips a termite mound open with its claws, blows the loose soil away with its nose closed, then sucks the insects in through the gap in its teeth. There is no reliable range-wide count of sloth bears; IUCN lists the species as Vulnerable with a decreasing trend across India, Sri Lanka and Nepal.
