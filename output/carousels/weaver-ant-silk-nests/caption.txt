Weaver ants (Oecophylla smaragdina) build their nests by sewing living leaves together with silk from their own larvae.

Workers hold the leaf edges in place while others carry a larva to the seam and pass it back and forth like a shuttle. Because they are not limited to existing tree holes, one colony can grow to about half a million workers spread over many trees, and the ants bite and spray formic acid to defend them.
