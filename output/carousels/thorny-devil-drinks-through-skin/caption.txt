The thorny devil (Moloch horridus) drinks through its skin, wicking water along grooves between its scales to its mouth.

This small ant-eating lizard lives in the sandy deserts of inland Australia, where rain is rare. Once its skin channels are full, it drinks with small gulping jaw movements, taking in roughly 0.7 microlitres per gulp in lab tests. The same study found that dew and condensation alone probably cannot fill the channels, so rain and damp sand matter most. It is listed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List (2017).
