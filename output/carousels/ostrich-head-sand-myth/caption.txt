Ostriches do not bury their heads in sand: the common ostrich (Struthio camelus) lies flat or dips into its nest instead.

The nest is a communal scrape in the ground shared by several hens. The male usually sits on the eggs at night and the females take turns by day, and every so often a bird lowers its head to turn them. Chicks use the lie-flat trick too, and if cornered an adult can deliver a dangerous kick with its two-toed feet.
