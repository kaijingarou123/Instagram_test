The platypus (Ornithorhynchus anatinus) lays eggs, finds prey by electricity and, if male, carries venom in its ankles.

It lives in the rivers and streams of eastern Australia and Tasmania, feeding mostly from dusk to dawn on insect larvae and freshwater crustaceans. Its body runs cool for a mammal, at about 32 °C. After a feed it squeezes back into a burrow so narrow that the entrance presses water out of its fur.
