The 'alpha wolf' was a product of captivity: wild gray wolf (Canis lupus) packs are families led by their parents.

The dominance-ladder picture came from studies of unrelated wolves housed together. Wolf biologist L. David Mech compared applying those results to wild packs with studying human family life in a refugee camp. In the wild pack he followed, the mother mostly led pup care and defence, and the father mostly led hunting and travel.
