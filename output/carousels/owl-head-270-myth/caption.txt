Owls cannot turn their heads all the way round: barred owls (Strix varia) and their relatives manage about 270 degrees.

An owl's eyes are fixed in bony tubes, so the whole head has to move. A Johns Hopkins team used angiography and CT scans on a dozen snowy, barred and great horned owls to see how the neck survives it. One surprise: the vertebral artery enters the neck higher than in other birds, at the 12th vertebra rather than the 14th, which gives it extra slack.
