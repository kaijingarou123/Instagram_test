A blue whale (Balaenoptera musculus) diving to feed can slow its heart to just 2 beats a minute.

Scientists fixed a suction-cup ECG tag to a single free-diving blue whale in Monterey Bay, California, and recorded 8.5 hours of heartbeats. During each feeding lunge at depth, its heart rate rose about 2.5-fold before slowing again. Their paper's title sums it up: extreme bradycardia and tachycardia in the world's largest animal.
