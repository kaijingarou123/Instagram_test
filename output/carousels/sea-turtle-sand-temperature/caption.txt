Green sea turtle (Chelonia mydas) hatchlings get their sex from sand temperature, and warmer beaches mean more females.

A green turtle lays about 110 eggs per nest, and they incubate in the sand for about two months, so a hotter summer can tip a whole season's hatchlings towards female. On the northern Great Barrier Reef, that tip has lasted more than two decades.
