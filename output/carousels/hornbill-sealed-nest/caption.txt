The female great hornbill (Buceros bicornis) seals herself inside a tree cavity for around four months.

She closes the entrance with droppings, leaving a slit just wide enough for the male to pass fruit through, and goes through a full moult while inside. The pair need big, old trees with natural cavities, and the loss of those old-growth nest trees is one of the main threats behind its Vulnerable listing.
