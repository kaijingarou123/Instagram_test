# Wildlife & Nature Education on Instagram — Content Strategy Research

Researched 2026-10-15. Scope: a new account posting locally generated carousels (1080x1350) and Reels (1080x1920) from CC-licensed photos + designed text, published via API. Where sources conflict, the conflict is noted rather than papered over.

## 1. Top-performing accounts and what they do

| Account | What works | Caption style | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| @natgeo (~280M) | Single hero images and short clips shot by ~110 commissioned photographers; the photographer, not a brand voice, writes the caption. Most-liked posts are emotional animal moments: Ami Vitale's ranger comforting Sudan, the last male northern white rhino; Paul Nicklen's crocodile "Niño"; the Kermode "spirit bear". Each featured post topped 750k likes+comments. | First-person field story, 2–5 sentences, photographer @handle, no hard sell. | Multiple posts daily. |
| @bbcearth | Clip-led Reels from documentary footage, one behaviour per post, with a short explanatory caption (e.g. elephant/egret symbiosis). | One-line "what you're seeing" + one science sentence. | Daily. |
| @nature_org, @usinterior, @joelsartore (Photo Ark) | Species fact + photo; Photo Ark posts a studio portrait + species status. | 1–2 fact sentences, conservation status, photographer credit. | Daily–several/week. |
| @natureismetal (~7–8M) | Aggregated predation/"nature is brutal" clips; growth came in viral bursts (+200k followers in single weeks in 2022); engagement ~1.2–1.8%. | Deadpan one-liners; tagline "Persist to Exist". | Daily. |
| Small fact pages (@animalfun_facts, @animalfactfiles) | Daily "did you know" text-on-image posts; low follower counts, which shows the format alone does not win — hook + visual quality does. | Fact + emoji + hashtags. | Daily. |

Patterns that recur across winners:
- **One idea per post.** Big accounts never stack five facts on one image; the carousel is the place to stack.
- **Named individuals** (Sudan, Mr. Blue the wolf, "Niño") outperform anonymous species shots — story over taxonomy.
- **Credit in-caption** ("Photo by @…") is universal and does not depress engagement.
- A peer-reviewed study of 670 images from 160 conservation accounts (Shaw et al., *Animals* 2022) found the species' taxon and presence of humans did **not** affect engagement; natural backgrounds and no-human framing dominated the sample. Implication: you can profile "unglamorous" species without losing reach if framing and hook are strong.

Hook patterns observed: question ("Why does this bird…"), surprising number ("1,260 heartbeats a minute"), reversal ("This is not a fish"), scale ("Its tongue is longer than its body"), stakes ("Fewer than 1,000 left").

## 2. Format benchmarks 2025–2026

**Engagement by format (compare within one study only; definitions differ):**
- Socialinsider 2025 (35M posts, engagement/followers): carousels 0.55%, Reels 0.52%, single images 0.37% (images down 18% YoY). 2026 update: carousels ~0.50%, images ~0.33%.
- Buffer (engagement/reach): carousels 6.9%, images 4.4%, Reels 3.3%; Reels reach ~36% more people than carousels and ~2.25x single images.
- CollabKit 10k-post study (Jan–Jun 2026, interactions/impressions): carousels 1.38%, Reels 1.23%, images 0.72%; **mixed carousels (stills + a short video slide) highest at 2.33%**; Reels under ~90s had higher median reach than longer.
- Rival IQ all-industry median engagement fell 17% to 0.30% in 2025 — expect low absolute numbers; benchmark against yourself.

**Takeaways:** Reels for discovery (non-follower reach), carousels for saves/engagement. Static single images are the weakest format in every dataset.

**Carousels:** platform limit is 20 slides (Aug 2024). No study gives a universal optimum; practitioner consensus is 5–10, with 6–8 the sweet spot to maximise completion. Slide 1 is the hook (it is the only slide shown in feed); **slide 2 should also stand alone** because Instagram re-serves carousels to non-swipers starting at slide 2. "Swipe for more" arrows still work but should be a subtle visual cue, not the headline. Last slide = CTA (save/share/follow).

**Reels:** max 3 minutes (Jan 2025). Length data conflicts: Socialinsider (140k business Reels) found 45–60s had the highest engagement rate and median views; a smaller study found 15–30s best engagement-per-view; the "7–15s = 60–80% retention" claim is unsourced. Practical rule: 15–35s for single-fact Reels, 45–60s for mini-explainers; the first 1–2 seconds must contain the on-screen hook.

**Save/share drivers:** saves come from reference value (lists, comparisons, "how to identify"); shares come from content that makes the sender look interesting (surprising fact, "send this to someone who…"). Vendors suggest targeting saves/impressions >1% for educational content.

## 4. Caption formula

**Structure:** `[Hook line — repeats or extends slide 1, ≤125 chars so it shows before "…more"]` → `[Body: 2–4 sentences, one new detail not on the slides, plain language, searchable keywords]` → `[Source line: "Source: IUCN Red List 2025" or a short cite]` → `[CTA: "Save this for your next pub quiz" / "Send to someone who loves octopuses"]` → `[Photo credit]` → `[3–5 hashtags]`.

**Hashtags (verified):** On 18–19 Dec 2025 Instagram's @creators account announced a **cap of five hashtags per post/Reel** (rolling out gradually; replaces the 30 limit). Mosseri has repeatedly said hashtags categorise content but do not materially boost reach. Use 3–5, all specific (#axolotl #amphibians #wildlifefacts), in the caption, not the first comment.

**Keywords/search (verified):** Instagram's official "Breaking Down How Instagram Search Works" states that search text is matched against **usernames, bios, captions, hashtags and places**, with popularity (clicks, likes, shares, follows) as a secondary signal. Since July 2025, public posts from professional accounts (18+) are also indexed by Google/Bing by default. So: species common name + scientific name + topic words in the caption and on-screen text; a keyworded handle/name ("… | Wildlife Facts") helps.

**Alt text:** Instagram supports custom alt text per image (Advanced settings). The in-app limit is commonly reported as 100 characters, though reports since 2021 say longer text is accepted; the Graph API `alt_text` field documents a 1,000-character limit. Write 1–2 descriptive sentences including the species name — it serves screen readers and is another text signal.

## 5. Design conventions

**Typography on a 1080-wide canvas** (phone renders it at ~1/3 scale, so 30 px ≈ 10 pt on screen). No official minimum; guides range from 24–36 px body. Recommended floor for this system: **body ≥ 36 px, sub-heads 48–60 px, hook headline 80–120 px, caption/credit footer ≥ 28 px**. Max ~25 words per slide; ≤ 2 lines for the hook. Bold geometric or humanist sans (Inter, Manrope, Outfit, Montserrat); one display face max.

**Carousel safe zone (1080x1350):** keep text ≥ 100 px from all edges; feed crops nothing at 4:5, but profile grid (now 3:4) crops top/bottom ~45 px each side, so keep the hook centred.

**Reels safe zone (1080x1920) — verify on a phone; guides disagree:** creator-consensus figures are **top 250 px, bottom 320–340 px, right 120 px** (username/audio at top; caption + action rail at bottom/right). Hopper's reading of Meta's percentage guidance is stricter: top 14% (~270 px), bottom 35% (~670 px), sides 6% (~65 px), bottom-right 40% rail over the rightmost ~230 px, leaving a ~950x980 px centre. Rule for this system: all hook text inside **x 80–880, y 280–1500**; nothing critical in the bottom 420 px or right 230 px.

**Contrast:** WCAG 4.5:1 minimum for body, 3:1 for ≥ 60 px headlines; put text on a 40–60% dark gradient scrim or solid panel, never directly on busy fur/foliage. Avoid pure white on pale sky.

**Palettes used by nature accounts:** deep forest green (#1B4332) + cream (#F1EDE4) + amber accent (#E9A23B); ocean navy (#0B2545) + ice (#E8F1F2) + coral (#FF6B57); earth brown (#3E2C1E) + sand (#EAD7B7) + moss (#7A8C5C). Pick one per account and keep a fixed accent for the hook word/underline.

**Slide layouts (carousel):** S1 full-bleed photo + 2-line hook + small "swipe →" chip; S2–S(n-1) alternating "photo top / text panel bottom" and "stat slide" (one giant number + one line); last slide "Sources + Save/Share" with a photo and the handle. **Reels:** 2–4 clips or Ken Burns stills, each 3–6 s; caption-sized on-screen text in centre band; progress bar optional; end card with handle and "Follow for daily facts".

## 6. Fact sourcing and accuracy

Tier 1 (cite by default): **IUCN Red List** (https://www.iucnredlist.org — status, population, trends; cite as "IUCN 2025, The IUCN Red List of Threatened Species, Version 2025-x" or by assessor; API v4 at https://api.iucnredlist.org needs a free token), **Animal Diversity Web** (https://animaldiversity.org — peer-edited species accounts, measurements, lifespan), **Encyclopedia of Life** (https://eol.org — traits, aggregates), **Cornell Lab / All About Birds** (https://www.allaboutbirds.org), **Smithsonian National Zoo** (https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals), **NOAA Fisheries species pages** (https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species-directory), **Wikispecies** (taxonomy).
Tier 2 (lead, then verify): **Wikipedia** — use the cited primary paper, not the article; WebFetch found internal inconsistencies (bee hummingbird 5 vs 6 cm; pangolin "most trafficked" is an estimate).

Rules: every numeric claim gets a source URL in the production record; prefer ranges to single numbers; say "estimated" when the source does; never upgrade "among the most" to "the most"; conservation status always from IUCN with assessment year.

**Citing without killing engagement:** big accounts put the source as a quiet footer on the last slide ("Sources: IUCN Red List 2020; Nowoshilow et al. 2018, Nature") and a one-line "Source:" in the caption above the hashtags. Never put citations on the hook slide. Pin a comment with full links when a post travels.

## 8. Content system

### Weekly mix (new account, first 90 days)
| Day | Format | Pillar |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Carousel (7 slides) | Species Spotlight |
| Tue | Reel (20–30 s) | Scale / "did you know" |
| Wed | Carousel (6 slides) | Myth-bust or X vs Y |
| Thu | Reel (30–45 s) | How does it survive? |
| Fri | Carousel (6–8 slides, incl. 1 video slide) | Conservation status / story |
| Sat | Reel (15–20 s) | Quiz ("guess the animal", answer in caption) |
| Sun | Carousel or Reel (rotate) | Weird-nature list |

4 Reels : 3 carousels; Stories daily reposting the day's post + a poll. Post same hour daily (test 7–9 am and 6–8 pm local to target audience). Review weekly: saves/impressions, shares/reach, follows per 1k reach, Reel 3-second hold rate.

### Pre-post scoring rubric (0–10 each; publish at ≥ 42/60, no criterion < 5)
| Criterion | 10 looks like | 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Hook strength | Specific number/reversal/question in ≤ 10 words; works with no context | Generic "amazing facts about X" |
| Visual quality | Sharp, ≥ 2000 px source, subject fills frame, licence verified, no watermark | Soft/cropped, busy background behind text |
| Factual accuracy with source | Every claim traced to Tier-1 URL; status + year from IUCN | Wikipedia only, no primary check |
| Originality / edit value | Comparison, framing or visual that isn't on page 1 of Google; designed stat slide | Fact copied from a listicle verbatim |
| Readability | Body ≥ 36 px, ≤ 25 words/slide, 4.5:1 contrast, inside safe zones | Text near edges or over fur |
| Caption/hashtag compliance | ≤ 5 specific hashtags, keywords in first line, source line, credit, alt text set | 10+ hashtags, no credit, no alt text |
